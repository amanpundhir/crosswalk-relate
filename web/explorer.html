Crosswalk explorer

Crosswalk checks whether a company is ready for a security audit. Here it checks Harbor & Pine, a made-up company, against NIST security standards. Every number is computed live in your browser.

What do these words mean?
Requirement
One rule a standard asks you to meet, such as "use multi-factor login". A framework (NIST 800-171, 800-53, CSF 2.0) is a set of them.
Control
Something the company does or uses to meet a requirement, such as an MFA setting.
Evidence
Proof that a control works, such as a report or screenshot. It has an expiry date.
Receipt
The chain from a requirement to a control to its evidence. Every claim in an answer comes with one.
Claim check
Re-runs each receipt against the graph and drops any claim that fails.
Covered, inherited, gap
Covered: met directly, with valid evidence. Inherited: met through a matching requirement in another framework. Gap: neither.
Demo date
The demo's "today". Expiry is judged against it, so results never change with the real date.

Start here: three things to try

Loading the graph database (Relate) in your browser…

Each circle is a requirement, control, piece of evidence or person. Lines show how they connect. A receipt is one chain of them.

      Pretend evidence expires

      Pick a piece of evidence to treat as expired on the demo date, . Coverage, What breaks and the Agent log update right away. Nothing is saved; reload to undo.

      Pretend a person leaves

      Pick someone to treat as gone. Controls they alone own will have no owner. Coverage won't change. Nothing is saved; reload to undo.

      Fictional data. Harbor & Pine is an invented company. Its people, systems, evidence and dates are made up for this demo; the framework text is NIST's and the policy wording comes from an open-source policy template set. Nothing here is compliance advice.

      Runs entirely in your browser: Relate 1.2.0 (WebAssembly) and Cytoscape.js 3.35.0, both vendored. What-if changes live in memory only. Reload to undo.
